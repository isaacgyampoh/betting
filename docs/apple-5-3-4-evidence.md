# What GameClaw does, factually

**Purpose.** This document exists for a qualified lawyer to assess GameClaw against Apple App Store
Review Guideline 5.3.4 and applicable Ghanaian law. It describes the implementation as built, from
the source, and **draws no legal conclusion**. Nothing here is an opinion about whether GameClaw
constitutes gambling, facilitates it, or requires a licence. That assessment is the reader's.

Every claim below was checked against the code on 2026-10-30 and cites where to verify it.

---

## 1. The guideline in question

> **5.3.4** Apps that offer real money gaming (such as sports betting, poker, casino games, horse
> racing) or lotteries must have necessary licensing and permissions in the locations where the app
> is used, must be geo-restricted to those locations, and must be free on the App Store.

Source: <https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/>

Note that Guideline 5.3 does **not** require the developer to be a legal entity rather than an
individual. That requirement appears in 5.4 (VPN apps) and 5.5 (MDM), not here.

## 2. What the app presents to a user

| Feature | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Live scores and fixtures** | Football fixtures, live minute-by-minute scores, league tables, lineups and match statistics, from API-Football. No betting involvement. |
| **Match detail and analysis** | Per-match statistics, recent form split home/away, and a written analysis generated by a language model from those statistics. |
| **Claw chat** | A private conversation with a language model. The user describes what they want; the model answers with football reasoning and can assemble a list of selections. |
| **Predictions / suggested selections** | The model proposes specific match outcomes with a stated estimated probability and the bookmaker's own price. The app states on screen that these are estimates, not certainties. |
| **Booking-code loading** | The user pastes a bookmaker's booking code; the app reads that coupon from the bookmaker and displays its selections and odds. |
| **Booking-code conversion** | The app finds the same games and markets at a second bookmaker, prices them there, and can ask that bookmaker to issue a new booking code for them. |
| **Favourites / following** | Teams and matches the user follows, with optional match alerts. |

## 3. Does the app accept or process wagers?

**No.** Verified by inspection:

- There is **no deposit, withdrawal, wallet, balance or stake-amount** mechanism anywhere in the
  codebase. A search across `lib/` and `app/api/` for `deposit`, `withdraw`, `wallet`, `wager` and
  `placeBet` returns one match, and it is a comment recording that a 2026-10-16 bookmaker test was
  "a non-wagering round trip".
- The app never asks for a stake. A booking code carries **selections and odds only**; the stake is
  entered by the user at the bookmaker, after GameClaw is out of the picture.
- No payment processor is wired to anything bet-related. Paystack exists in the schema for
  **subscriptions**, and the production API currently reports `"payments": false` — see
  <https://gameclaw-api.onrender.com/api/v1/health>.
- GameClaw holds no customer funds and pays out nothing. There is no settlement, no winnings
  calculation, and no record of whether a bet won.

## 4. What the sportsbook integration actually does

Two bookmakers are connected: **SportyBet Ghana** and **Football.com Ghana**. Both run the same
underlying sportsbook platform. The app calls four public endpoints on each
(`lib/bookmakers/live/hopegaming.ts`):

| Call | Effect |
|---|---|
| `GET /factsCenter/event`, `GET /factsCenter/pcUpcomingEvents` | Read fixtures, markets and odds. Read-only. |
| `GET /orders/share/{code}` | Read an existing booking code's selections. Read-only. |
| `POST /orders/share` | **Create a booking code** at that bookmaker from a list of selections. |

The fourth is the only one that writes. **This is the material fact for an assessment.** A booking
code is a coupon saved on the bookmaker's own system: it records which selections a person intends
to bet on. It carries no stake and no money. To place a bet, the user must open the bookmaker's own
app or site, enter the code, enter a stake, and confirm — all under the bookmaker's account,
identity checks and terms.

Both endpoints are the bookmaker's public web API, reached with no account and no credentials. They
were found by observing the public sites; see `docs/capturing-bookmaker-requests.md`.

The wider bookmaker catalogue (`lib/bookmakers/catalogue/africa.ts`) records each operator's
licensing regulator — for Ghanaian operators, the **Gaming Commission of Ghana**.

---

## 5. Does the app link users out to sportsbooks?

Yes, in two places:

- The bookmaker catalogue stores each operator's public website URL.
- A booking code is shown with the bookmaker's name so the user knows where to enter it.

The app's own share text includes `https://www.sportybet.com/gh/`
(`gameclaw_app/lib/features/share_import/shared_text.dart`).

---

## 6. Commission, affiliate or referral arrangements with bookmakers

**None found.** A search for `affiliate`, `commission`, `utm_`, `btag` and `clickid` across the
bookmaker and service layers returns no tracking parameters, no affiliate identifiers and no
revenue-share mechanism. The only matches for "commission" are the string **"Gaming Commission of
Ghana"**, the regulator's name in the operator catalogue.

Outbound links carry no tracking parameter that would attribute a signup or a bet to GameClaw.

**GameClaw's revenue model as built** is user-paid subscriptions: `Plan` rows with a price in
pesewas granting daily AI request allowances (`prisma/schema.prisma`). Payments are **currently
disabled in production** (`"payments": false`), so at the time of writing the app takes no money
from anyone, for anything.

## 7. Age restriction and geography

- **18+.** The API refuses every football endpoint until the user confirms they are 18 or older
  (`requireAdult`, `lib/auth.ts`). This is enforced server-side, not merely displayed.
- **Self-exclusion** exists: a user can block betting features until a chosen date, and the period
  cannot be shortened (`excludedUntil`, `prisma/schema.prisma`).
- **No geo-restriction is implemented.** The app is not currently limited to any territory by IP,
  store region or any other mechanism. The connected bookmakers are Ghanaian, and the public APK is
  distributed worldwide via a GitHub release.

Guideline 5.3.4 requires geo-restriction for apps it covers. **If the guideline applies, this is a
gap that must be closed before submission**, and it is a build change, not a legal one.

## 8. Open questions for the lawyer

These are the questions the technical facts cannot answer:

1. Does creating a booking code at a licensed sportsbook — a coupon with no stake, which the user
   must separately fund and confirm at that bookmaker — constitute "offering real money gaming"
   under 5.3.4, or facilitating it?
2. If so, which licences are required, in which territories, and must GameClaw hold them, or does
   the licensed operator's own licence suffice for a tool that merely composes coupons?
3. What does Ghanaian law — the Gaming Act, 2006 (Act 721) and the Gaming Commission's rules —
   require of a service that proposes selections and creates coupons but takes no stakes?
4. Does the model-generated prediction feature carry obligations of its own, separately from the
   coupon creation?
5. Does distributing the Android build outside Google Play change any of the above?

---

## 9. What has deliberately not been done

No functionality has been removed, hidden or renamed in response to this question. The app has not
been described to Apple in any submission. No licence, approval or legal position has been claimed
anywhere in the product or its metadata.

If the assessment concludes the guideline applies, the known build-side work is: geo-restriction to
licensed territories, an age rating declaring Gambling, and review notes stating plainly that the
app creates coupons and takes no wagers.
